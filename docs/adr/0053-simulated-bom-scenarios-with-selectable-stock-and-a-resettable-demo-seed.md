# Simulated BOM Scenarios with Selectable Stock and a Resettable Demo Seed

* Status: accepted
* Deciders: Development Team
* Date: 2026-11-10

Technical Story: [AI01] Prolog Bill of Materials and Stock Requirements

## Context and Problem Statement

AI01 must calculate the Bill of Materials of ordered pizzas before a complete ordering workflow exists. Simulated orders may be predefined or generated by the back end, and the client clarified (06/11/2026) that the calculation is independent of pending or confirmed orders, that it uses the current stock of the system at the time of the execution, that simulated or previously saved stock may be used for controlled, reproducible evaluations, and that a calculation never changes stored stock. The demonstration must show sufficient and insufficient stock and an aggregate partly available in stock, with documented initial data and expected results, and simulated scenarios must be reproducible or resettable. Actual and simulated inputs must follow the same rules and interfaces. We must decide where the simulated orders and stock live, how a calculation chooses between real and simulated stock, and how the demonstration data is created and reset.

## Decision Drivers

* No dependency on an ordering workflow that does not exist yet.
* The same business rules, snapshot format and calculation for real and simulated inputs.
* Reproducible results that do not change when someone adjusts the real stock.
* Stored stock, including the stock of a scenario, is never changed by a calculation.
* Demonstration data that can be reset without touching real data.

## Considered Options

* Simulated orders and stock inside the request body sent by the client
* Versioned AI scenarios stored by the back end, with a stock mode chosen per calculation, and an idempotent demonstration seed
* Temporary changes to the real stock during the demonstration, restored by hand

## Decision Outcome

Chosen option: "Versioned AI scenarios stored by the back end, with a stock mode chosen per calculation, and an idempotent demonstration seed".

* **AI scenario** (collection `AI BOM Scenarios`): `code` (unique, `^[A-Z0-9-]{1,64}$`), `name`, `description`, `version`, `simulated: true`, `orders [{ pizzaId, sizeId, quantity }]` (predefined pizzas and their sizes only) and `stock [{ productId, quantity }]` or `null` (a scenario may have no stock of its own), `isActive`. The domain rules of `ai-scenario.domain.js` validate the code, the orders and the stock (stock-controlled products only, integer quantities ≥ 0, no duplicates).
* **Stock mode, chosen per calculation** (`stockMode`):
  * `scenario`: the stock of the scenario, exactly as stored. A scenario without stock answers 400 `SCENARIO_WITHOUT_STOCK`; a stock-controlled item missing from it is reported as `MISSING_SCENARIO_STOCK` by the calculation.
  * `live`: the real stock at the time of the execution, for every active stock-controlled product (0 when it has no stock record, which is the rule of the stock endpoints).
* Both modes produce the same snapshot format (`stock[]`, `meta.sources.stock.source` `scenario` or `backend-live`) and the same calculation. The scenario snapshot is never cached, and any snapshot with a scenario is a simulation (`simulation: true`, `scenario { id, name, version, stockMode }`, `inputs { snapshotId, sources }`).
* The snapshot without `scenarioId` is unchanged, so the existing consumers of `GET /ai-data/snapshot` are not affected.
* **Demonstration seed** `npm run seed:ai01-demo -- --confirm` (`backend/scripts/seed-ai01-demo.js`, data in `backend/seed/ai01-demo.json`):
  * fixed ids (`de01…`) and the `[Demo] ` prefix on every document, so the demonstration data is easy to recognize and never mixed with real data;
  * it writes nothing without `--confirm`, validates the whole data with the domain rules before writing, and never overwrites a document that is not a demonstration document;
  * running it again restores the demonstration documents (products, aggregates, pizzas, real stock of the `[Demo]` products and the four scenarios `DEMO-BASIC`, `DEMO-SUFFICIENT`, `DEMO-INSUFFICIENT`, `DEMO-PARTIAL`) to their initial values. This is how a scenario is reset.
* There is no endpoint to create or edit AI scenarios; they are prepared by the seed (or a migration) and read by the back end only.

### Positive Consequences

* The calculation can be demonstrated without real orders, and the same scenario can be calculated with its own stock (reproducible) or with the real stock (current situation).
* The expected results of the demonstration are fixed and documented, and the httpyac flow checks them automatically.
* The real stock and the stock of the scenarios are never written by a calculation; a reset is an explicit run of the seed.

### Negative Consequences

* Scenarios are prepared by a script, not by a screen or an API; a new scenario requires a change to the seed data.
* Each scenario holds a single simulated order; generated or multi-day orders are not supported.
* The demonstration data lives in the same database as the real data (recognizable by the prefix and the fixed ids), so the seed must be run with care on shared databases.

## Pros and Cons of the Options

### Orders and stock in the request body

* Good, because there is nothing to store.
* Bad, because the client would become the source of business data, bypassing the rules and the versioning of the back end ([ADR-0039](./0039-planning-snapshot-as-the-data-contract-with-the-ai-service.md)), and results would not be reproducible from a known scenario.

### Stored scenarios, stock mode per calculation, idempotent seed

* Good, because simulated and real inputs follow the same rules and format and the scenarios are versioned and resettable.
* Bad, because scenarios can only be changed through the seed data.

### Temporary changes to the real stock

* Good, because no new model is needed.
* Bad, because it changes stored stock, which AI01 and the client explicitly forbid, and the demonstration could not be repeated safely.
